# Draft: request for isolated human kidney perfusion data

Status: unsent draft, prepared 2026-09-08. Sending requires user authorization. No donor or participant identifiers requested. Additional ex-vivo evidence only; not a replacement for healthy in-vivo validation.

Source: correspondence block in [Weissenbacher et al., DOI 10.1111/ajt.14932, PMC6491986](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6491986/). Published contacts: Constantin C. Coussios, `constantin.coussios@eng.ox.ac.uk`; Peter J. Friend, `peter.friend@nds.ox.ac.uk`. These are publication contacts, not confirmation of current availability.

Subject: Request for de-identified renal perfusion time-series — DOI 10.1111/ajt.14932

Dear Professors Coussios and Friend,

We are developing a research simulation of renal water and sodium handling and would like to evaluate a narrowly defined ex-vivo model against your study, “Twenty-four-hour normothermic perfusion of discarded human kidneys with urine recirculation.” We would not treat this as validation of healthy whole-body physiology.

Would you be able to share the de-identified, per-kidney numeric data underlying the urine-flow figures, together with paired perfusate and urine sodium measurements and their exact sampling/collection times? Published kidney numbers alone are sufficient; we do not request personal or donor identifiers.

For reproducible boundary conditions, we would also need, where available:

- Initial circulating volume and perfusate composition, renal pressure/flow and temperature traces, and any measured filtration reference.
- Times, volumes and composition of fluid replacement, nutrition and other boluses/infusions, plus sample withdrawals, leaks and urine recirculation status.
- Units, collection interval definitions, missing-data flags, instrument accuracy and the meaning of reported uncertainty, including whether each SD describes temporal variation or between-kidney variation.

Could you also clarify the urine-recirculation status of kidneys 12 and 13? Supplement Table S3 says “no,” while the main text and supplementary figure captions describe recirculation. We would appreciate clarification of the 620 ml “average” urine output in section 3.2.8 versus the 1985 ml mean in Table 3.

If these data can be shared, please specify the permitted use, citation and redistribution terms. We would like to retain exact source data and publish a derived validation fixture in an open-source repository, but would do so only under terms you authorize. A partial dataset or a pointer to an existing repository would also be helpful.

Thank you for your time.
